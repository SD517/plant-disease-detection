How LeafCheck works

LeafCheck uses transfer learning. A network that already learned general shapes, edges and textures from millions of ImageNet photos (MobileNetV2 or ResNet50) is adapted to recognise 38 crop and disease combinations from the PlantVillage dataset.

Training in two steps

First only the new classification layer is trained while the pre-trained layers stay frozen. Then the top layers of the backbone are unfrozen and fine-tuned with a very small learning rate so the model adapts without forgetting what it knows.

Why the heat-map?

The coloured overlay is a Grad-CAM map. It shows where the network found evidence for its answer. If the warm colours sit on the background instead of the leaf, do not trust the result.

Limits you should know

  • PlantVillage photos were taken on plain backgrounds in controlled light. Field photos with soil, hands or other plants can be misread.
  • The model only knows 38 classes. A crop or disease outside that list will be forced into the closest one.
  • It does not check whether the picture is a leaf at all. Always read the confidence score.
  • Advice shown is general. Use it as a starting point and confirm with an expert.
